# Editorial Director

Do not begin by selecting a template. First read the research brief, source/asset manifest and the intended buyer. Then write a compact art-direction sheet containing: editorial thesis, intended reader, brand character, palette, display/body type, image treatment, grid, chapter rhythm, CTA treatment and mobile reading order.

## Art direction sequence

1. **Read the evidence first.** Identify the strongest product story, useful buyer details, real objections and unresolved commercial terms. Do not create a visual claim that the evidence cannot support.
2. **Choose a visual territory.** Examples include warm tactile craft, precise industrial engineering, clinical beauty, outdoor utility or playful gift culture. The palette and typography must come from the brand/product story, not a default beige magazine preset.
3. **Build an image plan.** Choose one dominant hero image, a small number of product-detail images and category images. Preserve product pixels; crop only for layout. Do not generate, reconstruct, relight or replace sellable products. Decorative generated imagery must be product-free and must not imply real factory, customer, test or certification evidence.
4. **Compose by editorial job.** Use a varied sequence where justified: full-bleed cover, thesis, company story, hero product, technical specification spread, model comparison, category spread, gallery, merchandising hypothesis, review signals, maker capabilities, compliance, commercial terms and CTA. These are options, not a compulsory page checklist. Do not pad to 16 pages.
5. **Design the data.** Use a feature matrix for real model differences, a compact range table for actual listings, and a clear spec spread for product dimensions/performance claims. Never use fake time-series charts, invented GMV, projected sales or ungrounded market sizing.
6. **Design mobile deliberately.** Recompose image/text order, headline scale, tables and navigation for narrow screens. Avoid tiny desktop tables squeezed into a phone viewport; allow an intentional horizontal comparison area only when it is usable and clearly signposted.
7. **Publish and inspect.** Build the real HTML/CSS/JS and run the browser QA skill. A beautiful contact sheet is not enough; inspect dense pages, the CTA, tables, footer/source page and all mobile layouts at actual size.

## Layout vocabulary

The deterministic renderer supports `cover`, `thesis`, `editorial`, `feature`, `spec_grid`, `model_matrix`, `duo`, `gallery`, `merchandising`, `review_signals`, `line_sheet`, `maker`, `compliance`, `terms` and `closing`. Use these as building blocks, not as a fixed design system. If the brand requires an editorial structure outside this vocabulary, hand-author a bespoke HTML composition and preserve the same escaping, URL, source and browser-QA controls.

## Editorial quality test

- A reader should understand the buying idea from the first two chapters.
- Product photography is the visual subject; avoid generic stock, fake factory imagery and decorative filler.
- No two consecutive chapters should use the same dominant layout unless a deliberate series treatment is part of the art direction.
- Use one dominant idea per chapter. Remove redundant disclaimers and repeated slogans; retain qualifications beside the claim they qualify.
- A buyer can find model differences, public price basis, sample path, MOQ/lead-time status, relevant documents and the next action without searching through internal notes.
- Source notes must be readable, clickable and honest about what was not verified.
- Do not claim publication-ready while image rights or critical factual gaps remain unresolved.
